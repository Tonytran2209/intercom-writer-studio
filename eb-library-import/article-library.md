## Shared rules (hold across nearly all articles)

| Element | Rule | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Opening pull-quote | Open with a one-line quote or tagline in bold/italic or a blockquote, before any body text. It can be a known quote or a house line. | 6 of 7 (only Chill Space skips it) |
| Voice | First-person plural "we", warm and casual. Humour comes through **parenthetical asides** ("Spoiler: …", "you know who you are 👀", "just keep in mind not to overwater!"). | 7 of 7 |
| Company name | "F. Learning Studio", "F.Learning" or "F" / "F-ers" / "team F". Informal short forms are common in event recaps. | 7 of 7 |
| Real names | Teammates are named, with a role or trait, only when the input names them. | Color (4 named), Birthday (2 named + role) |
| Images | Images appear between paragraphs or sections. Event recaps use several images in a row. | 7 of 7 |
| Length | Event recaps run ~150–300 words. Explainer and workshop pieces run ~350–500 words. | — |

## Article types (pick one in Step 3)

### Type A — F Environment
*What it covers:* the working environment at F.: spaces, setup, where and how the team works day to day.
*Reference: "Chill Space, Chill Mind: The Mini Guide to Office Relaxation Zones" (Jun 24 2025, Team Culture)*
- **Title:** "[Catchy phrase]: The [descriptor] Guide to [topic]"
- **Intro:** 2 paragraphs. The first states the company philosophy; the second moves into the theme ("Let's take a quick tour…").
- **Body:** one H2 per place or instance, written as "Name: descriptive subtitle". Each section runs sensory description → how/why the team uses it → image. Three sections.
- **Close:** an H2 titled "Why [X] Matters", reflective, naming values (balance, curiosity).
- **No pull-quote.** This is the only article without one.

### Type B — Culture & events
*What it covers:* culture shown through events, including culture workshops and occasions (birthdays, sport, Mid-Autumn, a playful team session).
*References: "F's Badminton Madness: Smashing Deadlines (and Shuttlecocks)" (Jun 13 2025) · "Color Me Impressed: Production Team's Color Playtime 🎨✨" (Jun 13 2025) · "MAY BIRTHDAY CELEBRATION 🎉" (May 30 2025). All Team Culture. Occasion pieces such as "Scraped Knees, Crowded Streets, and Lanterns: Our Mid-Autumn Fun" sit under Employee Engagement on the hub.*
- **Title:** a pun or double meaning that ties the activity to work ("Smashing Deadlines (and Shuttlecocks)"), often with an emoji. The plain ALL-CAPS event name is also used, though rarely.
- **Hook:** the pull-quote, then one line of contrast (deadlines → play).
- **Body:** little or no H2 structure. The story runs in time order: what happened → who was there → a funny or telling detail → how it ended. The birthday piece uses ✨ bullets for the people and a "🍰 What went down" list of 3.
- **Close:** a forward-looking sign-off to the team ("Same time next week?", "Until the next splash of color, stay inspired, team F!", "Can't wait for the next birthday bash! ❤️"). It may add one reflective line ("it's these simple, heartfelt moments…").
- **Tone:** the most playful of the three types. Self-aware jokes are welcome.
- **Workshops:** a workshop belongs here when it is mainly playful team bonding (Color Me Impressed). A workshop that mainly teaches a skill is Type C2.

### Type C — Knowledge of F / F is Learning
*What it covers:* how F. learns and shares knowledge, either as a recurring habit or system (C1) or through a knowledge workshop where a skill or method was taught (C2).

**C1 — Learning practice**
*References: "Your Co-Workers Are Basically Google with a Better Sense of Humor" (Dec 30 2024) · "Keep It, Share It, Use It: How We Embraces Knowledge Retention at F.Learning" (Dec 24 2024). Both Team Culture.*
- **Title:** either a humorous analogy stated as fact ("…Basically Google with a Better Sense of Humor") or a triad followed by "How We [verb] [practice] at F.Learning".
- **Hook:** the pull-quote.
- **Body:** 3–4 short H4 sections in a Why → How → What progression ("Why Keeping Knowledge Matters" / "How We Build…" / "What We Do…"). One section may hold a bold-label bullet list of concrete assets. A "Picture this: …" scenario is also used.
- **Close:** either a recruiting call-in section ("Are You One of Us?", describing who would fit) or no separate close; the last section ends the piece.

**C2 — Knowledge workshop**
*Reference: "Pitch & Win: Boosting Your Sales and Consulting Game" (May 8 2025, Team Expertise), a consultative-selling workshop.*
- **Title:** an alliterative or rhythmic phrase, then the benefit ("Pitch & Win: Boosting Your…").
- **Hook:** a pull-quote stating the principle, then a positioning intro ("we don't just pitch — we consult").
- **Body:** H2 "Rethinking the approach" → H2 listing numbered techniques (bold sub-heads, each with a humorous aside) → H2 "Lessons from Recent Cases", with 2 bullet cases in the form situation → what we did → **bold lesson**.
- **Close:** one line reframing the skill as a way of connecting ("not just about improving how we work, it's about improving how we connect").

### Choosing the type
| Input looks like | Type | Usual category |
|---|---|---|
| Spaces, setup, how the workplace looks and works | A | Team Culture |
| An event, occasion, or playful team workshop | B | Team Culture / Employee Engagement |
| A recurring learning or knowledge-sharing habit | C1 | Team Culture |
| A workshop where a skill or method was taught | C2 | Team Expertise |
